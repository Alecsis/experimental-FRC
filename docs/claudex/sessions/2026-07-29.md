### Root cause, confirmed (two-stage chain)

- **Stage 1 — initiator.** `OpenLoopVoltage` maps desired module speed to voltage kinematically with no load
  compensation. Measured/commanded speed is **0.539** within the first 0.5 s, while tracking error is still only
  ~0.03 m — i.e. under-delivery precedes the error rather than following from it.
- **Stage 2 — amplifier (newly identified brownout loop).** Translation `kP = 5` converts ~0.7 m of error into a
  ~3.5 m/s correction → command saturates → four modules pin at max → **265 A mean / 711 A peak** →
  `/SystemStats/BatteryVoltage` collapses **13.5 V → 8.46 V** → each module can no longer exceed ~4.8 m/s → error
  grows → demand grows.

### New finding — Disturbance Report **Finding 4 resolved** (this is the session's most durable result)

The severe 2.0 m run has always been the outlier: largest disturbance, *best* numbers (mean error 0.10 m, best
speed delivery). This session adds the missing piece — its **feedback magnitude also stays low (~0.9 m/s** vs
5.8–6.7 m/s elsewhere), in both experiment arms.

Mechanism: `Trajectory/Error*` is computed **estimator-pose vs setpoint**. `setSimulationWorldPose()` moves the
physics body without informing the estimator, and the 2 m displacement then exceeded
`Constants.kMaxVisionJumpMeters = 1.0`, producing the **242 consecutive vision rejections** the Disturbance
Report already measured. The estimator was therefore locked out from ever observing the truth — the robot sat
2 m off-path while the error signal read near zero and the controller stayed calm.

**The severe run is not evidence of disturbance recovery. It is evidence of _blind_ tracking**, and it ties the
anomaly directly to the F5 vision-lockout gap. The Disturbance Report flagged this as needing a re-test after the
chassis-PID fix; it is answered without one.

## Remaining unknowns

- **Where the initial error comes from.** The feedforward plan demands only **1.04 m/s** mean and is fully
  achievable, yet error appears within the first ~1.5 s and drives feedback to **5.6–6.4× feedforward**. Nothing
  tested so far explains this. It is now the sharpest open question.
- **Under-delivery is mode-independent.** The robot delivers only ~45–65 % of requested chassis speed in *both*
  control modes, with voltage headroom available (median applied/bus **0.43–0.45**), no persistent bus limit, and
  zero traction violations in the plan.
- **Leading remaining hypothesis (untested):** measured slip is present (median wheel/ground **1.11**, **40.3 %**
  of samples > 1.15), so maple-sim's propelling-force clamp may bind on the *total* commanded force including
  feedback — i.e. the robot is slip-limited by over-commanding, not by the plan.
- **Scope limit on experiment 2, stated honestly:** it tested closed-loop velocity with the *untuned CTRE
  template* gains (kP = 0.1), per the no-tuning constraint. That rules out "flipping the request type is
  sufficient." It does **not** prove properly characterized gains wouldn't help. It does mean the repo's standing
  Velocity-migration recommendation rests on an unproven benefit rather than a demonstrated one.
- All previously carried-forward unknowns are unchanged: chassis-PID divergence bug, `AutonomousHealthMonitor`
  threshold tuning, F5 backoff design, Layer 4 trigger threshold.
